Japanese konbini phrases

The konbini (7-Eleven, Lawson, FamilyMart) is where you'll speak the most Japanese on any trip. The staff ask the same few questions every time, so once you know them, checkout takes ten seconds.

What staff ask, and how to answer

  • 袋はご利用ですか？ふくろはごりようですか · fukuro wa go-riyō desu ka?Would you like a bag?The politer way staff ask. Bags cost a few yen.
  • お箸おつけしますか？おはしおつけしますか · ohashi otsuke shimasu ka?Shall I put in chopsticks?
  • ポイントカードはお持ちですか？ポイントカードはおもちですか · pointo kādo wa o-mochi desu ka?Do you have a point card?大丈夫です or a small head shake is fine.
  • お支払いは？おしはらいは · o-shiharai wa?How will you pay?
  • カードでお願いしますカードでおねがいします · kādo de onegaishimasuBy card, pleaseSwap カード for 現金 (cash) or 電子マネー.
  • レシートは大丈夫ですレシートはだいじょうぶです · reshīto wa daijōbu desuI don't need the receipt
  • おにぎりonigiriRice ballPull tab 1, then corners 2 and 3, and the seaweed stays crisp.
  • ATMē-tī-emuCash machineKonbini ATMs, especially 7-Eleven's, usually take foreign cards.

Questions

What does the konbini staff say when I walk in?

いらっしゃいませ (irasshaimase): “welcome in”. It's not a question, so you don't need to answer. A small nod is plenty.

How do I say no thank you at a konbini?

大丈夫です (daijōbu desu). It works for the bag, the chopsticks, heating your food and the point card. A small shake of the head works too.

Can I use a foreign card at a konbini?

Usually, yes, for paying and at the ATM. 7-Eleven ATMs are the most reliable for foreign cards.
