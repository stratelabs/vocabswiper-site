Spanish false friends

Spanish and English share thousands of words from Latin, and most of them mean what they look like. These 20 are the exceptions: words that look like English but mean something else. A few of them, like embarazada, have caused some very confusing conversations.

Questions about Spanish false friends

What is a false friend?

A false friend is a word that looks or sounds like a word in another language but means something different. Teachers also call them false cognates. The danger is that they feel safe: you don't think to check a word you already “know”.

Which Spanish false friend causes the most trouble?

Embarazada. It means pregnant, not embarrassed. “Estoy muy embarazada” announces a baby. For embarrassed, say avergonzado (or avergonzada), or “me da vergüenza”.

How do you avoid false friends?

Learn words in sentences instead of guessing from how they look. When a new word looks like English, check it once. Most of the time it really is the same, which is exactly why the exceptions catch people out.
